Lesson 13

2 Corinthians, Part 1

God displays Christ’s power through weak, suffering servants who minister with integrity and reconciliation.

Primary text2 Corinthians 1–7
FocusPower in Weakness

The thread through chapters 1–7

Corinth valued impressive speakers, visible strength, and public credentials. Paul points instead to God-given sufficiency, Spirit-transformed lives, endurance through suffering, and reconciliation through Christ. The messenger is fragile so that the power is unmistakably God’s.

2 Corinthians 1:3–2:13

Comfort, integrity, and restoration

God strengthens suffering believers so they can comfort others. Paul answers accusations honestly, and church discipline moves toward forgiveness, comfort, and restored love when repentance appears.

2 Corinthians 2:14–3:18

New covenant ministry gives life

God makes servants competent for a new covenant in which the Spirit gives life, removes the veil in Christ, and transforms believers into the Lord’s image.

2 Corinthians 4:1–18

Treasure in jars of clay

Christ’s treasure is carried in fragile servants so God’s extraordinary power—not the messenger’s polish—becomes visible through endurance and faithful witness.

2 Corinthians 5:1–7:16

Hope, reconciliation, and holy allegiance

Resurrection hope produces courage and accountability. Christ reconciles sinners and sends them as ambassadors who pursue holiness, godly sorrow, and openhearted relationships.

Central question

How does God display Christ’s power through weak and suffering servants?

Contemporary significance

The Gospel Then and Now

Church pressure then

Strength and credentials defined ministry

The Corinthians were tempted to judge ministry by impressive credentials, visible strength, and freedom from suffering.

Church temptation now

Polish can masquerade as power

We can confuse platform, growth, charisma, or constant success with the presence and power of God.

Gospel-shaped ministry

Christ’s life appears in fragile servants

The Spirit transforms hearts, and reconciled people minister with integrity, endurance, holiness, and open hearts.

Discuss

  1. What makes Christian ministry credible when visible success and faithfulness do not appear together?
  2. How does the image of treasure in jars of clay challenge Christian celebrity and platform culture?
  3. How can Christ’s judgment seat encourage responsibility without destroying assurance?
  4. What does it mean for an ordinary Christian to serve as Christ’s ambassador of reconciliation?
  5. How can we distinguish ordinary contact, unhealthy influence, a binding yoke, and direct participation in idolatry?

Before Lesson 14

2 Corinthians, Part 2
