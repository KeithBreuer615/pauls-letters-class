Teacher resources

Teach the text, trace the gospel

These resources are public and designed to help every teacher preserve the series’ central approach while teaching in his or her own voice.

Public resource notice

This page is intentionally public. Do not post prayer requests, contact information, pastoral concerns, private feedback, or copyrighted commentary excerpts here.

Shared teaching commitments

What every lesson should accomplish

1

Locate the text

Show where the passage fits within the whole Bible, the whole letter, and Paul’s developing argument.

2

Name the church problem

Identify the concrete pressure, distorted belief, or disordered practice Paul addresses in that congregation.

3

Trace the gospel

Ask what God has done in Christ and how that truth confronts, corrects, or sustains the church.

4

Address the church today

Identify the contemporary form of the same temptation and describe the shared life the gospel creates among us.

The Gospel Then and Now

This is a recurring movement, not an application appendix: church problem then → enduring human temptation → church expression now → gospel answer → faithful life together. Begin with what Paul actually addresses, distinguish the enduring principle from its ancient form, and ask first how the gospel reshapes a congregation before narrowing the discussion to individual behavior.

Teacher GuideLesson 1

Read the Letter Before Borrowing the Verse

Primary exercise: Philemon 1–25. Supporting texts: 1 Corinthians 15:1–4; 2 Peter 3:15–16; Colossians 4:16.

Teaching guide with notes (PDF)Teaching script (PDF)Participant handout (PDF)
0–3
Opening prayer

Ask the Holy Spirit to guide and teach the class.

3–8
Hook

We are reading someone else’s mail—written for us.

8–15
Series theme

One gospel; different churches, cultures, questions, and crises.

15–23
Reading framework

Whole Bible → Whole Letter → Passage; then faithful response.

23–36
Philemon lab

One reader reads the whole letter; the class distinguishes stated facts, inferences, and unknowns.

36–46
Discussion

Trace how the gospel reshapes the shared life and witness of Philemon’s house church.

46–50
Close and prayer

Restate the master question and assign the readings for Lesson 2.

Teaching emphasis

Paul does not paste the same paragraph into every letter. He shows how the same gospel answers each church’s particular problem. The class should learn to follow his thought rather than merely collect favorite verses.

Certainty ladder for Philemon

Stated

Paul appeals for Onesimus, sends him back, calls him a brother, and offers to cover a debt.

Inferred

Onesimus may have run away or wronged Philemon—but Philemon 18 is conditional.

Unknown

How Onesimus met Paul, exactly what happened earlier, and how Philemon responded.

Contemporary significance

A church that makes reconciliation visible

Then

Receive him as a brother

Paul’s appeal will be heard and embodied within a house church, not hidden inside a private spiritual experience.

Now

Old status still ranks people

Churches can let usefulness, reputation, wealth, education, or past failure determine whose presence and voice matter.

Gospel answer

Grace creates family

Because Christ receives indebted sinners, the church’s relationships must display shared belonging and costly reconciliation.

Teacher GuideLesson 2

Saul Before Paul

Primary texts: Acts 7:54–9:19; Galatians 1:11–24; Philippians 3:4–11. Aim: trace Saul’s movement from religious certainty and violence to helplessness, grace, and reception by Christ’s church.

0–3
Welcome and prayer

Ask the Spirit to teach and correct us.

3–6
Frame the lesson

Can sincere religious certainty still resist God?

6–19
Stephen and Saul

Acts 7:54–8:4: seeing, refusing, violence, prayer, and scattering.

19–21
Summarize Acts 8

Persecution scatters the church; the gospel spreads.

21–36
Christ and Ananias

Acts 9:1–19: confrontation, grace, helplessness, and reception.

36–44
Paul interprets

Galatians shows God’s initiative; Philippians shows a new accounting.

44–48
The Gospel Then and Now

Apply the problem corporately to the church.

48–50
Close

Summary, next reading, and prayer.

Keep the hour focused

Do not let harmonizing every detail in Acts or debating Saul’s two names displace the main movement: the risen Christ takes the initiative, and the church receives the former persecutor.

Contemporary significance

When good gifts become grounds for superiority

Then

Confidence in the flesh

Saul’s religious résumé and zeal became a basis for confidence and destructive opposition to Christ’s people.

Now

Church credentials become idols

Doctrine, tradition, politics, reputation, service, and success can become ways a church ranks people and justifies exclusion.

Gospel answer

Grace redirects every gift

Identity and righteousness are received in Christ; knowledge, tradition, experience, and zeal become gifts for serving his whole church.

Teacher GuideLesson 3

Paul’s Calling and Mission

Primary texts: Acts 9:19–31; Acts 13:1–12; Romans 15:14–33. Aim: trace how Christ redirects Saul’s zeal, how the Spirit sends through the church, and how congregations partner in Christ’s mission.

Welcome and prayer

Ask the Spirit to guide, teach, and send the church.

Recap Lesson 2

Grace confronted Saul, created a brother, and redirected his zeal.

Calling in action

Acts 9:19–31: new people, message, pattern, opposition, and suffering.

Bridge to Antioch

Spirit and church send

Acts 13:1–12: worship, discernment, synagogue witness, and opposition.

Romans 15: grace, frontier, partnership, and prayer.

41–47
The Gospel Then and Now

How does a congregation participate in Christ’s mission?

47–50
Synthesis and prayer

Review, assign Galatians and Acts 15, and pray.

Keep the hour focused

Paul’s apostolic calling is unique, but the church’s worship, discernment, sending, prayer, and support remain instructive. Keep maps and travel details subordinate to the mission’s purpose.

Contemporary significance

When mission becomes someone else’s work

Then

A worshiping church sends

Antioch discerns together, obeys the Spirit, sets people apart, sends them, and remains a partner in the mission.

Now

Churches turn inward

Mission can become an optional program, a task outsourced to specialists, or a success story measured mainly by activity and survival.

Gospel answer

Christ’s mission orders church life

The whole congregation participates through worship, discernment, sending, prayer, giving, welcome, teaching, and formation.

Teacher GuideLesson 4

Galatians, Part 1

Primary texts: Galatians 1–2; Acts 15. Aim: state the Galatian crisis clearly and connect justification by faith with the church’s shared table and shared belonging.

0–5
Opening question

When does adding a religious requirement actually change the gospel?

5–14
The crisis

Identify what was being added and why Paul responds so urgently.

14–25
Gospel source

Trace divine revelation and public recognition in Galatians 1–2.

25–36
Truth of the gospel

Connect justification by faith with Jew–Gentile fellowship.

36–46
The Gospel Then and Now

Identify unwritten entrance requirements and ways church conduct can contradict gospel doctrine.

46–50
Close

No added entrance requirement may replace faith in Christ.

Keep the hour focused

State your working chronology briefly. Do not let North-versus-South Galatia or every debate over “works of the law” consume the lesson.

Contemporary significance

When church culture adds to Christ

Then

An added entrance requirement

Gentiles were pressured to adopt Jewish identity markers, and table separation treated them as less than full members.

Now

Unwritten tests of belonging

Politics, class, ethnicity, education, worship preferences, or denominational background can quietly divide first- and second-class believers.

Gospel answer

One gospel, one table

Justification by faith creates a shared life in which every believer stands by the same grace and belongs through the same Christ.
