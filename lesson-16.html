Lesson 16Romans, Part 2

From Condemnation to Life in the Spirit

United with Christ and indwelt by the Spirit, believers have new life, assurance, and hope despite sin and suffering.

Series locationLesson 16 of 24
Primary textsRomans 5:1–21; 6:1–14; 7:24–8:4; 8:14–39

Follow Paul’s argument

  1. Romans 5:1–11: Justification brings peace with God, access to grace, and hope in suffering.
  2. Romans 5:12–21: Christ becomes the representative head of a new humanity ruled by grace and life.
  3. Romans 6:1–7:25: Union with Christ gives a new identity; the good law cannot rescue people ruled by sin.
  4. Romans 8:1–39: The Spirit gives freedom, adoption, hope, help, and assurance.

Central line

In Christ: a new standing. With Christ: a new identity. By the Spirit: a new life and a sure hope.

Two representatives, two humanities

Adam’s trespass opens the reign of sin, condemnation, and death. Christ’s obedience opens the reign of grace, justification, and life. The gospel does not merely improve Adam’s old humanity. God joins believers to Christ and gives them a new standing and identity.

United with Christ

Believers have been joined to Christ’s death and resurrection. Grace changes more than the verdict; it changes our realm, master, and direction. Christian obedience flows from an identity received, not acceptance earned.

Life in the Spirit

Romans 8 gathers the argument into God’s gifts: freedom from condemnation, adoption as God’s children, hope amid groaning, the Spirit’s help in weakness, and assurance that nothing in creation can separate Christ’s people from God’s love.

Contemporary significance

The Gospel Then and Now

Church pressure then

Sin and suffering remained real

Believers needed to understand what justification produces while weakness, suffering, and death still pressed upon them.

Church temptation now

Feelings become the verdict

We can treat salvation as pardon only, read suffering as abandonment, or make comfort rather than conformity to Christ the measure of God’s goodness.

Gospel-shaped church

The Spirit creates secure, hopeful people

Those joined to Christ fight sin from a received identity, groan with hope, and trust the Father’s purpose and inseparable love.

Discuss

  1. How is fighting sin from a secure identity in Christ different from fighting sin to earn God’s acceptance?
  2. What does Romans 8 allow Christians to say—and not say—when someone is suffering?
  3. When feelings of assurance fluctuate, what objective grounds for confidence does Paul give?
  4. Which gift in Romans 8—freedom, adoption, hope, help, or assurance—does the church most need to recover?
