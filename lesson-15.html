Lesson 15Romans, Part 1

God Justifies the Ungodly

All people stand guilty under sin, but God reveals his righteousness by justifying sinners through faith in Jesus Christ, leaving no room for boasting.

Series locationLesson 15 of 24
Primary textsRomans 1:16–17; 3:9–26; 4:1–5, 16–25

Follow Paul’s argument

  1. Romans 1:16–17: The gospel reveals God’s righteousness and saves everyone who believes.
  2. Romans 1:18–3:20: Every group stands under sin; every mouth is silenced before God.
  3. Romans 3:21–31: God justifies by grace through faith because Christ has dealt with sin.
  4. Romans 4:1–25: Abraham shows that the promise is received by faith and belongs to all who believe.

Central line

No one has a case to present before God, but everyone may receive the gift God provides in Christ.

Every mouth is silenced

Romans 1–3 unfolds like a courtroom argument. Humanity exchanges the Creator’s glory for idols. The moral critic condemns others but cannot excuse the same sin. The person who possesses God’s law cannot claim that hearing it is the same as obeying it. Paul’s destination is that Jew and Gentile alike are under sin, every mouth is silenced, and the whole world is accountable to God.

God’s saving action in Christ

The words “But now” in Romans 3:21 announce the turn. God acts freely for people who cannot establish a claim upon him. Justification is God’s gracious verdict, grace is his free action, redemption is Christ’s liberation of sinners, and the sacrifice of atonement shows that forgiveness does not ignore evil. God is both just and the one who justifies those who have faith in Jesus.

Abraham believed God’s promise

Abraham was not paid righteousness as wages for work. He trusted God, and righteousness was credited to him before circumcision and centuries before the Mosaic law. He therefore becomes the father of both Gentile and Jewish believers. His story challenges pride because no one earns the promise, and it challenges despair because the promise depends on God’s power and faithfulness, not ours.

Contemporary significance

The Gospel Then and Now

Church pressure then

Every group claimed an escape

People could point toward another group while resisting the truth that all stand accountable before God.

Church temptation now

We build new ladders of merit

Religious knowledge, moral comparison, politics, heritage, achievement, or shame can become reasons for pride or despair.

Gospel-shaped church

One gracious verdict creates one family

Christ deals with sin, and everyone who trusts God’s promise receives the same gift and belongs to the same family of faith.

Discuss

  1. Why does Paul take so much time to show that every group is under sin?
  2. How can God forgive guilty people without becoming unjust?
  3. Why is faith not simply another work that earns God’s approval?
  4. How does Abraham’s story challenge both religious pride and hopelessness?
