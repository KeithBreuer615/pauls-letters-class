Lesson 9

1 Corinthians, Part 1

The crucified Christ overturns worldly measures of wisdom, power, and status. Church leaders are servants, the church belongs to God, and every believer belongs to Christ.

Primary textsActs 18:1–17; 1 Corinthians 1–4
FocusThe Cross and a Divided Church

A different scorecard

Corinth’s culture prized wealth, ancestry, patrons, education, impressive speech, and visible success. Paul answers the church’s rivalry by returning every leader, gift, and ministry to Christ crucified.

1 Corinthians 1:1–17

Called by grace but divided

God called the church by grace, but believers were using Paul, Apollos, Cephas, and Christ as competing badges of superiority.

1 Corinthians 1:18–2:5

The cross overturns worldly wisdom

Christ crucified overturns human boasting and every attempt to measure God’s work by status, performance, or rhetorical brilliance.

1 Corinthians 2:6–3:4

Maturity has visible fruit

The Spirit reveals God’s wisdom, while jealousy, quarreling, and personality-centered factions expose continuing immaturity.

1 Corinthians 3–4

Servants, builders, and stewards

Leaders plant and water, but God gives growth. Christ is the foundation, the congregation is God’s temple, and every steward answers to the Lord.

Central question

How does the cross change the way Christians evaluate wisdom, leadership, maturity, and success?

Contemporary significance

The Gospel Then and Now

Church reality then

Boasting produced division

The Corinthians ranked leaders and one another by eloquence, wisdom, status, baptism, and visible success.

Church temptation now

Preferences become identities

Favorite pastors, theological tribes, political identities, worship preferences, education, wealth, programs, and church size can become substitutes for identity in Christ.

Gospel-shaped church

Everyone belongs to Christ

The cross removes boasting. Leaders serve, God receives credit for growth, maturity appears in humble love, and the church remembers that it belongs to Christ.

Discuss

  1. How do we decide whether a church or Christian leader is successful? Which parts of our scorecard would Paul challenge?
  2. What evidence of spiritual immaturity does Paul identify? How can we use his words as a mirror rather than a weapon?
  3. When does healthy appreciation for a pastor or teacher become unhealthy factional loyalty?
  4. How can theological, political, or worship preferences become stronger identities than belonging to Christ?
  5. What qualities should we seek in leaders who serve God’s church?

Before Lesson 10

1 Corinthians, Part 2

Read 1 Corinthians 5–7. Mark the problems Paul addresses concerning church discipline, lawsuits, sexuality, marriage, divorce, and singleness. Notice how often Paul returns to the truth that believers belong to Christ.
