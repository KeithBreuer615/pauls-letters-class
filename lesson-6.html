Lesson 6

1 Thessalonians, Part 1

The gospel creates a faithful community whose changed life becomes a witness even under pressure.

Primary textsActs 17:1–10; 1 Thessalonians 1–3
FocusThe gospel received under pressure

What makes faith visible?

The Thessalonians received the gospel amid opposition. Their work of faith, labor of love, endurance of hope, and witness beyond their congregation made God’s transforming work visible.

Acts 17:1–10

The gospel arrives under pressure

Paul proclaims Jesus as the Christ in Thessalonica. Some believe, but opposition quickly forces Paul and Silas to leave.

1 Thessalonians 1

Faith made visible

The church receives the word with joy in the Holy Spirit, turns from idols, becomes a model, and makes the Lord’s message ring out.

1 Thessalonians 2:1–12

A message supported by a life

Paul’s ministry is marked by courage, integrity, gentleness, affection, hard work, and conduct worthy of the gospel.

1 Thessalonians 2:13–3:13

Blocked, not silenced

Separation and opposition do not stop the Word from working. Timothy’s report brings joy, and Paul prays for growing love and holiness.

Central question

What makes a church’s faith in Christ visible?

Contemporary significance

The Gospel Then and Now

Church reality then

A young church under pressure

The Thessalonians faced social pressure, political accusations, questions about Paul’s motives, and separation from their teachers.

Church temptation now

Measuring the wrong things

Churches can measure health by buildings, budgets, attendance, programs, comfort, reputation, or “our kind of people” while overlooking faith, love, hope, holiness, and witness.

Gospel-shaped church

A credible life and ringing witness

God’s Word works through the Spirit, producing credible lives, costly love, steadfast hope, and witness beyond the congregation.

Discuss

  1. What makes a church’s faith in Christ visible?
  2. Which signs of church health are easiest to confuse with gospel faithfulness?
  3. How did Paul’s conduct support his message?
  4. What would it mean for the Lord’s message to ring out from our congregation?

Before Lesson 7

1 Thessalonians, Part 2

Start here: Read 1 Thessalonians 4:1–18 and 5:1–11. Continue if possible: Finish chapter 5. Notice how believers live, grieve, wait, and encourage one another in hope.
