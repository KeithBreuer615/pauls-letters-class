Lesson 142 Corinthians, Part 2

Strength Made Perfect in Weakness

The grace of Christ creates generous people and faithful servants who boast not in themselves but in Christ’s power displayed through weakness.

Series locationLesson 14 of 24
Primary texts2 Corinthians 8:1–15; 9:6–15; 10:3–5; 11:1–33; 12:7–10; 13:5–14

Follow the movement

  1. 2 Corinthians 8:1–9:15: Grace produces willing generosity, transparent stewardship, and thanksgiving to God.
  2. 2 Corinthians 10:1–18: Gospel authority demolishes false arguments without worldly weapons or self-promotion.
  3. 2 Corinthians 11:1–33: Paul exposes false apostles and turns boasting into a catalogue of costly service and weakness.
  4. 2 Corinthians 12:1–13:14: Christ’s sufficient grace rests on weak servants, and apostolic authority builds up the church.

Central line

The gospel frees Christians to give without securing themselves, serve without promoting themselves, and remain faithful without pretending to be strong.

Grace-shaped generosity

Paul begins not with a budget but with God’s grace. Christian giving is willing, proportionate, sometimes sacrificial, directed toward genuine need, and rooted in Christ, who became poor so that sinners could receive salvation’s riches.

Visible procedures and multiple trusted representatives protect the gift, the leaders, the recipients, and the church’s witness. Sowing and reaping is not a prosperity formula: God supplies sufficiency so believers can abound in good works, meet needs, continue in generosity, and give thanks to God.

The cross overturns impressive ministry

Paul refuses the weapons of intimidation, manipulation, status, and polished self-promotion. The “super-apostles” offered impressive claims but proclaimed another Jesus, a different spirit, and a different gospel. Paul overturns their contest by boasting in costly service, suffering, and weakness.

The thorn and sufficient grace

Paul does not identify his thorn, so we should resist diagnosing it. What the text makes clear matters more: it was painful and persistent; Paul prayed repeatedly for relief; and Christ answered with sustaining grace. “My grace is sufficient for you” does not minimize pain or forbid prayer. It promises enough grace for faithful endurance when deliverance is delayed or denied.

Contemporary significance

The Gospel Then and Now

Church pressure then

Visible strength defined ministry

Corinth admired patronage, credentials, polished rhetoric, spiritual experiences, and public power.

Church temptation now

Image becomes the measure

We may measure ministry by platform, fundraising, charisma, growth, celebrity, or constant victory.

Gospel-shaped ministry

Grace is sufficient

Grace gives freely, practices integrity, accepts weakness, and uses authority to protect and restore.

Discuss

  1. How does generosity make the unity of Christ’s church visible?
  2. What keeps sowing and reaping from becoming a prosperity formula?
  3. What worldly measures of successful ministry still attract Christians?
  4. Why does Paul boast in weakness rather than simply refusing to boast?
  5. How can Christ’s sufficient grace encourage sufferers without minimizing their pain?
