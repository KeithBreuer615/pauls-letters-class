Lesson 11

1 Corinthians, Part 3

Christian freedom and spiritual gifts must be governed by love and used to build up the gathered church.

Primary text1 Corinthians 8–14
FocusLove Builds Up

The thread through chapters 8–14

Paul addresses food offered to idols, apostolic rights, the Lord’s Supper, spiritual gifts, and orderly worship. One question joins them: Will believers use knowledge, freedom, and gifts to elevate themselves or to build Christ’s people in love?

1 Corinthians 8:1–11:1

Freedom seeks another person’s good

Christian freedom refuses participation in idolatry, surrenders legitimate rights when love requires it, and considers gospel witness rather than merely asking what is permissible.

1 Corinthians 11:17–34

The Lord’s Supper makes one body visible

The meal proclaims Christ’s self-giving death. Receiving it carelessly while humiliating other believers contradicts what the bread and cup announce.

1 Corinthians 12–13

Many gifts serve one body

The same Spirit gives varied gifts to interdependent members for the common good. Without patient, truthful, enduring love, even impressive gifts amount to nothing.

1 Corinthians 14

Worship builds up through order

Gathered worship favors intelligibility, edification, self-control, testing, and order so believers are strengthened and outsiders can recognize God among his people.

Central question

How does love govern Christian freedom, the Lord’s Supper, and spiritual gifts in the gathered church?

Contemporary significance

The Gospel Then and Now

Church reality then

Status divided the body

The Corinthians used knowledge, rights, wealth, and visible gifts to distinguish themselves from other believers.

Church temptation now

Church life becomes self-assertion

We can turn freedom into self-assertion, Communion into routine, gifts into status, and worship preferences into contests.

Gospel-shaped church

Christ gave himself for one body

Believers surrender rights when love requires it, honor every member, and use every gift to build others up.

Discuss

  1. When can exercising a legitimate Christian freedom become unloving?
  2. How is a vulnerable conscience different from mere annoyance at someone else’s choice?
  3. How can a church contradict the Lord’s Supper even while using the correct words and elements?
  4. What happens when Christians treat a visible gift or ministry as proof of greater spiritual importance?
  5. Which Corinthian problem most closely resembles churches today: individual rights, social division, worship preferences, platform-seeking, spectacular gifts, or disorder?
  6. What would it look like for our congregation to measure worship and ministry by whether they build others up?

Before Lesson 12

1 Corinthians, Part 4

Read 1 Corinthians 15–16. Mark what Paul says happened to Christ, what will happen to believers, and why resurrection makes present service worthwhile.
