Lesson 7

1 Thessalonians, Part 2

Because Christ will return and raise his people, believers pursue holiness, love, hope, and watchful faithfulness.

Primary text1 Thessalonians 4–5
FocusHoly living and resurrection hope

Waiting for Christ is active

Resurrection hope does not pull believers out of ordinary life. It shapes how they use their bodies, love their neighbors, do their work, grieve, treat one another, test what they hear, and encourage the church.

1 Thessalonians 4:1–12

Holiness, love, work, and witness

God calls believers to sanctification and honor. Growing love and quiet, responsible work support a credible witness before outsiders.

1 Thessalonians 4:13–18

Grief transformed by the gospel

Because Jesus died and rose, deceased believers will rise and share fully in Christ’s coming. Christians grieve, but not without hope.

1 Thessalonians 5:1–11

Watchful faithfulness

The Day will arrive unexpectedly. Children of light stay awake through faith, love, hope, and mutual encouragement—not date-setting.

1 Thessalonians 5:12–24

A waiting church lives together

The church respects, warns, encourages, helps, prays, tests, and trusts the faithful God to sanctify his people.

Central question

How should Christ’s return change the way we live today?

Contemporary significance

The Gospel Then and Now

Church reality then

Pressure, grief, and uncertainty

Believers faced sexual pressure, bereavement, uncertainty about Christ’s return, disruptive behavior, and the ordinary strains of congregational life.

Church temptation now

Prophecy detached from discipleship

We can separate future hope from present holiness, grieve as though death has the final word, chase prophetic timetables, or let end-times disagreements divide Christ’s church.

Gospel-shaped church

Children of the coming day

Because Jesus died, rose, and will return, the church grieves with hope, lives in holiness and love, works responsibly, tests carefully, and encourages one another.

Discuss

  1. How should Christ’s return change the way we live today?
  2. What is the difference between grief and grief without hope?
  3. What does watchfulness look like without date-setting?
  4. Why must a church both welcome the Spirit’s work and test everything?
  5. Where do we need the promise, “The one who calls you is faithful, and he will do it”?

Before Lesson 8

2 Thessalonians

Start here: Read 2 Thessalonians 1 and 2:1–17. Continue if possible: Read the entire three-chapter letter. Notice how Paul addresses persecution, confusion about the Day of the Lord, and disorderly living.
