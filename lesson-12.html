Lesson 12

1 Corinthians, Part 4

Christ’s bodily resurrection guarantees the resurrection of his people and makes steadfast service worthwhile.

Primary text1 Corinthians 15–16
FocusResurrection Hope

The thread through chapters 15–16

Paul reminds the Corinthians of the gospel they received: Christ died for sins, was buried, was raised, and appeared to witnesses. Because the risen Christ is the firstfruits, his people will be raised and can serve him with confidence now.

1 Corinthians 15:1–11

The gospel received and witnessed

Christ died for our sins, was buried, rose on the third day, and appeared to many witnesses. The resurrection belongs to the gospel’s historical core.

1 Corinthians 15:12–28

Christ the firstfruits

If Christ has not been raised, faith is futile and sin still condemns. But Christ has been raised, guaranteeing the resurrection of his people and the defeat of death.

1 Corinthians 15:35–49

A transformed, embodied life

The resurrection body is continuous with the present body yet gloriously transformed: imperishable, powerful, and fully animated by God’s Spirit.

1 Corinthians 15:50–58; 16

Victory produces steadfast service

Because death will be swallowed up in victory, believers remain steadfast and abound in the Lord’s work through generosity, courage, strength, and love.

Central question

How does Christ’s bodily resurrection guarantee the resurrection of his people and make steadfast service worthwhile?

Contemporary significance

The Gospel Then and Now

Church reality then

Faith without future resurrection

Some Corinthians tried to retain Christian faith while denying that the dead will be bodily raised.

Church temptation now

Hope reduced to escape or symbol

We can treat resurrection as a metaphor, imagine salvation as escape from embodiment, or live as though our present work ends at death.

Gospel-shaped church

Embodied hope and faithful work

Because Christ was bodily raised, believers await transformed embodied life and serve now with steadfastness, generosity, courage, strength, and love.

Discuss

  1. Why does Paul treat bodily resurrection as essential rather than secondary?
  2. How does Christ as the firstfruits strengthen Christian hope?
  3. What misunderstandings are corrected by Paul’s phrase “spiritual body”?
  4. Where are Christians tempted to think faithful work is pointless or wasted?
  5. How should resurrection hope change the way the church serves now?
