Lesson 1Series Introduction

How to Read Paul’s Letters Well

Paul wrote real letters to real churches facing real problems—and he applied the same unchanging gospel to each changing situation.

Primary textPhilemon 1–25
Supporting texts1 Corinthians 15:1–4; 2 Peter 3:15–16; Colossians 4:16

Paul’s letters are…

  • Real correspondence: written by an apostle to particular churches and people.
  • Occasional: each letter arose from a specific occasion, question, conflict, or need.
  • Pastoral: Paul writes to form faithful people, not merely to transfer information.
  • Authoritative Scripture: God speaks through these ancient letters to the whole church.
  • Gospel-centered: what God has done in Christ governs what God’s people believe and do.

Questions to ask

  1. What do I know about the writer, recipients, setting, and occasion?
  2. What problem, question, or need is being addressed?
  3. How does this paragraph function in Paul’s developing argument?
  4. What does Paul say God has done, is doing, or will do in Christ?
  5. What response—belief, worship, obedience, endurance, or reconciliation—flows from that gospel truth?

Philemon reading lab

As you read, distinguish what the letter states, what can reasonably be inferred, and what remains unknown.

Discuss

  1. What exactly does Paul ask Philemon to do, and how does he support that appeal?
  2. Where do you see the gospel reshaping identity, power, debt, and relationship?
  3. What would be lost if we pulled one verse from this letter and ignored the rest?
